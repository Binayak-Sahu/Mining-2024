## Claims
| # | claim (paraphrase) | ref | excerpt (< 15 words) | location |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Five levels, in a preferred order by general effectiveness: elimination, substitution, engineering, administrative, PPE | niosh-hoc | "The preferred order of action based on general effectiveness is" | Overview |
| 2 | Elimination removes the hazard at the source; it is preferred because no exposure can occur | niosh-hoc | "preferred solution to protect workers because no exposure can occur" | Elimination |
| 3 | Elimination, substitution and engineering are more effective because they need little human interaction | niosh-hoc | "control exposures without significant human interaction" | Key points |
| 4 | Administrative controls and PPE can also reduce exposure | niosh-hoc | "can also be effective at reducing workers' exposures" | Key points |
| 5 | Substitution uses a safer alternative to the hazard source | niosh-hoc | "using a safer alternative to the source of the hazard" | Substitution |
| 6 | Compare a substitute's new risks with the original, including how it combines with other agents | niosh-hoc | "how the substitute will combine with other agents" | Substitution |
| 7 | Engineering controls reduce or prevent hazards contacting workers (barriers, ventilation, equipment changes) | niosh-hoc | "reduce or prevent hazards from coming into contact with workers" | Engineering controls |
| 8 | The best engineering controls are in the original design, block at the source, resist tampering, need minimal user action | niosh-hoc | "Need minimal user action for the controls to work" | Engineering controls |
| 9 | Engineering controls can cost more up front, but long-term costs tend to be lower | niosh-hoc | "long-term operating costs tend to be lower" | Engineering controls |
| 10 | Administrative controls set work practices that reduce duration, frequency or intensity of exposure (training, job rotation, rest breaks, access limits) | niosh-hoc | "reduce the duration, frequency, or intensity of exposure" | Administrative |
| 11 | PPE: don't rely on it alone when other options exist; effective only if used correctly and consistently; can be costly over time | niosh-hoc | "should not rely on PPE alone to control hazards" | PPE |
| 12 | PPE must be provided while other controls are developed or when they can't reduce exposure enough | niosh-hoc | "While other controls are under development" | PPE |

Option-level reasoning (derived from the definitions above): a machine guard is a physical barrier, so it is an engineering control (claim 7). Respirators are worn equipment, so they are PPE (claim 11). Warning signs change behaviour and leave the hazard in place, which is an inference from claims 2 and 10.
